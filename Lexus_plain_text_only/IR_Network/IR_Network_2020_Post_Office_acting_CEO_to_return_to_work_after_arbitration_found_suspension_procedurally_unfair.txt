Post Office acting CEO to return to work after arbitration found suspension procedurally unfair

Reported on: 6 October 2020

BL Premium reports that suspended SA Post Office (Sapo) acting CEO Lindiwe Kwele is preparing to return to work after an arbitration process found that her suspension had been procedurally unfair.  

Kwele was suspended in December just four months into the role, after the board received a whistle-blower’s report containing allegations that included conflicts of interest and irregular extensions of contracts.  She challenged her suspension in an arbitration process and the award last week found that she had been suspended for a fair reason but that the Sapo board had “fired the gun on [her] prematurely” when it announced her suspension before she was given a chance to provide reasons why this should not happen.  This was despite an undertaking by the board that it would give her 48 hours to provide the reasons.  The Sapo board said on Tuesday that it would continue with the internal disciplinary processes against Kwele, while it was also considering challenging the cost award in the arbitration matter.  In the award, the board was ordered to pay Kwele two months of her salary as acting CEO as compensation for the procedurally unfair suspension.  It was also ordered to pay 50% of all Kwele’s arbitration costs, including the cost of her legal representation.
