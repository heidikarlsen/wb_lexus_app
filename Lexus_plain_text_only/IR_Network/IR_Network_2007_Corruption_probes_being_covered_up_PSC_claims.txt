Corruption probes being covered up, PSC claims

Reported on: 29 January 2007

Cape Town - MPs were astonished to hear on Friday that some government departments were covering up investigations into corruption. 

The ad hoc committee reviewing the state institutions supporting democracy was told by Stan Sangweni, the chair of the public service commission (PSC), that this was happening.

Talking about the national anticorruption hotline, set up three years ago to receive confidential tip-offs on corruption, he said: "We have encountered a lot of frustration. We have proof that the relevant authority has covered these things up. It is a very serious matter."

Other commissioners explained that the PSC did not investigate corruption allegations phoned in, but passed the allegations on to the executive authority for investigation.

"We do experience a lot of problems with the hotline in respect of report backs from the ministries," said Admill Simpson, the deputy director-general in charge of investigations. "Perhaps it is the lack of investigating capacity." 

He added that there was unevenness across the entire government about the way the hotline was responded to, even between national departments.

Although generally satisfied that whistle-blowers who called the hotline were protected, Simpson said the Protected Disclosures Act was not sufficiently regulated. The justice ministry has not yet published regulations and guidelines.

The commission also noted that the number of service delivery complaints phoned in on the hotline had grown from 65 a year to 1 300, and the ability to investigate these had been hurt by budget constraints. 

The PSC told the committee it was unable to fight for more money because in order to preserve its independence, the PSC did not use the ministry of public service and administration to lobby for its needs.
