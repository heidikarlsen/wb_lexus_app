Punishing the disclosure of false info will discourage whistleblower‚ committee hears

Reported on: 15 September 2016

Prosecution and punishment for the “deliberate disclosure of false information featured high on the agenda” of the Portfolio Committee on Justice and Correctional Services on Wednesday.

Concerns were raised by the Open Democracy Advice Centre that a clause in the Protected Disclosures Amendment Bill‚ which makes “it an offence to disclose false information” would discourage whistleblowers.

According to the bill‚ a two-year prison sentence or a fine can be imposed for such an offence.

A statement from the committee said the rights group argued that the “clause goes too far‚ as it would create an additional concern for whistleblowers and therefore does not have a place in this legislation”.

It also told the committee that “the number of whistleblowers has been decreasing” and “suggested incentives should be considered‚ to encourage people to come forward”.

The Congress of South African Trade Unions indicated that it was in support of the bill.

It did‚ however‚ suggest a tightening the wording of the legislation to include “failed to have undertaken reasonable steps to verify the validity of the information‚ made the disclosure knowing information is false and with malicious intent and that the affected party consequentially suffered verifiable quantifiable harm or damage”.

The committee said the bill “aims to introduce a duty to inform employees or workers who have disclosed information regarding unlawful or irregular conduct”.

Its purpose is also to “provide for immunity against civil and criminal liability flowing from a disclosure of information that shows or tends to show that a criminal offence has been committed‚ is being committed or is reasonably likely to be committed”. – TMG Digital
