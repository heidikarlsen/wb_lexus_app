



20 General principles
The Law of South Africa (LAWSA)


LAWSA  >  The Law of South Africa (LAWSA)  >  Labour Law: Part 2  >  Resolution Of Labour Disputes  >  Unfair Labour Practices  >  Specific Cases Of Unfair Labour Practices  >  Occupational Detriment Suffered Because Of A Protected Disclosure (Whistleblowing)

20  General principles  The wrongful suffering of an occupational detriment (short of dismissal) by an employee because of making a protected disclosure in terms of the Protected Disclosures Act 1 constitutes an unfair labour practice.2 The purpose of the Protected Disclosures Act (PDA) is four-fold,3 namely:
(a) to protect an employee,4 having made a protected disclosure, from being subjected to an occupational detriment;
(b) to provide remedies to an employee who suffered any occupational detriment after making a protected disclosure;
(c) to provide a procedure for an employee to disclose improprieties by his or her employer; and
(d) to promote the eradication of criminal and other wrongful activities by state organs and private institutions and entities.5
  When applying the PDA a four-stage process (requirements) must be followed,6 namely:
(a) Determine whether the information is in fact a disclosure.7
(b) If it is, the next question is whether the disclosure is protected.8
(c) The next stage is to determine whether the employee was subjected to any occupational detriment.9 
(d) What remedy should be available in respect of the notorious treatment.10
