NPA confident of Phakoe murder conviction

Reported on: 3 April 2012

The National Prosecuting Authority in North West maintains that it has a strong case against the two accused in the murder of councillor and whistle-blower Moss Phakoe. Such talk comes after arguments by the defence counsel in a trial within a trial seek to determine the admissibility of evidence relating to the pointing out of crime scenes. 

The team argues that one of the accused, Enock Matshaba was forced to point out crime scenes without being accompanied by his lawyer. Former Rustenburg mayor Matthew Wolmarans and his former bodyguard and driver, Matshaba are standing trial for the murder of Phakoe, who was gunned down outside his house in 2009. 

North West NPA spokesperson Frank Lesenyego says: "We can say that infact all the witnesses who testified today corroborate what we have presented before the court of law, both the confession and the pointing out are admissible to the court of law. So really it's not within the court, it's within the law."

Phakoe was killed a few days after handing over documents of alleged corruption to then Co-operative Governance and Traditional Affairs Minister, Sicelo Shiceka. 

The court hearing resumed yesterday after a six-week postponement. It heard evidence from five police officers regarding the pointing out of certain scenes that relate to the crime. 

During the last testimony, a witness told the court that Wolmarans confessed to him that he paid two hit men R80 000 each to kill Phakoe. The courtroom was full to capacity. The case continues.
