



Poromano & another / Sage Aluminium CC [2012] 3 BALR 239 (MEIBC)
Arbitration Law Reports

Division: Metal and Engineering Industries Bargaining Council
Case No: MEWC 6490
Date: 28/09/2011
Before: S Bhana, Panellist
Keywords


Application in terms of section 191(5)(a)(iv) of the LRA
Bargaining councils — Jurisdiction — Council lacking jurisdiction to entertain dispute over short time under unfair labour practice jurisdiction.
Unfair labour practice — Short time — Dispute over short time not falling within ambit of statutory definition of “unfair labour practice”.

Editor’s Summary


The applicants accused the respondent of perpetrating an unfair labour practice against them by keeping them indefinitely on short time. They claimed that they had requested the respondent to retrench them, but that the owner had maintained that he could not afford to do so.

The commissioner noted after a hearing in the absence of the respondent that the only issue was whether the dispute concerned an alleged unfair labour practice. Even on the applicants’ own version, the matter did not fall within the terms of the statutory definition of that term. Short time is regulated by the council’s main agreement, and the applicants’ complaint related to the enforcement of that agreement. The matter had, accordingly, been referred in terms of the incorrect provision of the LRA.

The application was dismissed.

Award

Details of hearing and representation

1. Details of application and process

The matter was scheduled for arbitration on 20 September 2011. The respondent was absent and the applicants were present. The respondent was notified by fax on 14 July 2011 of the arbitration proceedings. There are no apparent reasons for its absence and I, being satisfied that it had been properly notified, proceeded with the arbitration. 

2. Issue to be decided

Whether the respondent had committed an unfair labour practice against the applicants?

 
[2012] 3 BALR 239 (MEIBC) at 240

3. Background to the issue

The respondent employs the applicants as an installer/fixer (“Poromano”) and assembler/machinist (“Svenson”) respectively. Poromano earns R9 200 per month and Svenson R49 per hour. The two applicants alleged that the respondent had committed an unfair labour practice in respect of short time against them. 

4. Survey of evidence and argument

The applicants testified that they had been on short time for the past two years and have not been working constantly. They had asked their employer, Jerome Moses, to retrench them but he refuses, claiming he could not afford it. They referred to two other employees of the respondent who concluded termination deals with the respondent for R5 000 and R1 800 respectively. They have no insight into the respondent’s finances or that the respondent really keeps the company afloat from his own finances. 

The applicants also explained how short time was effected and that there was a lack of proper procedures. They submitted payslips to support their claim of unfair short time. They had discussed the matter with the MEIBC agent but had not lodged a formal complaint. 

5. Analysis of evidence and argument

The crisp issue in dispute are whether the respondent had committed an unfair labour practice. In the absence of the respondent, the applicant’s version must be accepted as a true reflection of the events that had transpired. I had no reason not to believe their testimony. I will not repeat the whole of the evidence presented but will only refer to relevant parts thereof although I have considered all of the evidence.

It is imperative for any arbitrator or commissioner to first establish whether he or she has jurisdiction to deal with a dispute. This is especially true in this matter. Section 186(2) of the LRA defines what an unfair labour practice is and for clarity I include the full definitions here:

“‘Unfair labour practice’ means any unfair act or omission that arises between an employer and an employee involving –


(a) unfair conduct by the employer relating to the promotion, demotion, probation (excluding disputes about dismissals for a reason relating to probation) or training of an employee or relating to the provision of benefits to an employee;
(b) unfair suspension of an employee or any other unfair disciplinary action short of dismissal in respect of an employee;
(c) a failure or refusal by an employer to reinstate or re-employ a former employee in terms of any agreement; and
(d) an occupational detriment, other than dismissal, in contravention of the Protected Disclosures Act, 2000 (Act No. 26 of 2000), on account of the employee having made a protected disclosure defined in that Act”.

It is painfully clear from the evidence presented by the applicants and the definitions above, that the case before me does not constitute an unfair labour practice. This matter was referred to the CDR section of the MEIBC, who deals  
[2012] 3 BALR 239 (MEIBC) at 241
primarily with disputes arising from the LRA and are not part of the terms and conditions of employment as detailed in the industry main agreement. This section does, therefore, have no jurisdiction to deal with the dispute. 

It is important, however, to note that the issue of short time is included in the industry main agreement, specifically in part 1, section 7 of the main agreement. This section forms part and parcel of the conditions of employment. The dispute is, therefore, an enforcement matter and must be dealt with by the designated agents and the dispute resolution procedures relating to enforcement. 

6. Award

The dispute before me does not qualify as an unfair labour practice as defined in the LRA. 

The applicants may refer the matter as a formal complaint to the designated agents for processing through the enforcement of and compliance with the industry main agreement. 

No cases were referred to in the above award.
