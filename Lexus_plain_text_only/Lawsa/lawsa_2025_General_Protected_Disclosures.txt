The Law of South Africa (LAWSA)


LAWSA  >  The Law of South Africa (LAWSA)  >  Labour Law: Part 2  >  Resolution Of Labour Disputes  >  Unfair Labour Practices  >  Specific Cases Of Unfair Labour Practices  >  Occupational Detriment Suffered Because Of A Protected Disclosure (Whistleblowing)

22  General protected disclosure1  A general disclosure will be protected if it complies with the following requirements:2
(a) it is made in good faith by an employee;
(b) who reasonably believes that the information is substantially true;3
(c) which is not made for personal gain;
(d) if one of the following conditions4 applies:
(e) the employee, making the disclosure, has reason to believe that he or she may be subjected to an occupational detriment if he or she makes the disclosure to the employer;
(ii) the employee has reason to believe that evidence is likely to be destroyed if the disclosure is made to the employer;
(iii) the employee making the disclosure has previously disclosed the same information to his or her employer or a person prescribed in the Protected Disclosures Act;5
(iv) the impropriety is of an exceptionally serious nature;6
(e) if in all circumstances of the case it is reasonable to make the disclosure. In order to determine this, the following must be considered, namely:7
(i) the identity of the person to whom the disclosure is made;
(ii) the seriousness of the impropriety;
(iii) whether the impropriety is continuing;
(iv) whether the disclosure is made in breach of a duty of confidentiality of the employer towards any other person;
(v) the public interest;
(vi) whether the disclosure was made in a reasonable or fair manner and so forth.8
