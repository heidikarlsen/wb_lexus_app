                                                      Free State High Court Says Metsimaholo Municipality’s Termination of Employee Hopolang Lebusa as CFO Was Unlawful — Appeal Dismissed                            

                                R                                eported                                 on:                                 2                                7                                                                 November                                 20                                2                                5                  

                                                      Bloemfontein, 27 November 2025 – In a decisive blow to Metsimaholo Local Municipality, the Free State High Court has dismissed the municipality’s bid for leave to appeal against a ruling that declared the termination of former Chief Financial Officer Hopolang Ishmael Lebusa unlawful                                                      . The judgement, delivered today by Acting Deputy Judge President J.J. Mhlambi and Acting Judge D. Greyling-Coetzer, upholds the earlier decision from April 2025, stating that the appeal lacks reasonable prospects of success and                                             offers no compelling reasons for further review. This outcome reinforces the court’s view that the municipality’s actions violated principles of natural justice, employment laws, and its own internal rules, leaving the local authority facing hefty legal costs and questions about its governance practices.                  

                                            The ruling comes amid ongoing challenges for Metsimaholo, a municipality in the Free State province known for its industrial hubs like Sasolburg, where issues of financial management and procurement have long drawn scrutiny. With this appeal dismissed, Lebusa’s reinstatement as CFO appears set in stone, potentially disrupting recent leadership changes and highlighting the need for better accountability in local government.                  

                                            A Long-Running Dispute Rooted in Controversy                  

                                            The case traces back to July 2023, when Lebusa, appointed as CFO on 1 August 2022 under a five-year contract ending 31 July 2027, was placed on precautionary suspension along with other senior officials. This included the then Municipal Manager, Adv. Mofokeng, and three directors. The suspensions arose from serious allegations tied to a high-value security tender awarded earlier that year, aimed at protecting municipal buildings, assets, and staff.                  

                                            The tender process became a focal point of controversy, with claims of irregularities, favoritism, and possible corruption. A forensic investigation completed in mid-2023 implicated several officials, including Lebusa, suggesting flawed procurement steps and undue influence. Reports indicated that the winning bidder may have had personal connections to decision-makers, raising concerns about kickbacks and biased evaluations. This led to the removal of then-Mayor Jeff Zwane from the Democratic Alliance, who                                             was accused of involvement in the scandal, including appointing a bodyguard and driver linked to the tender. Local businesses in the area expressed outrage, arguing that the process sidelined qualified bidders and favoured outsiders, prompting calls for transparency and even legal challenges from affected companies.                  

                                            Lebusa denied the allegations, maintaining that his role in financial oversight did not involve direct manipulation. Despite this, on 8 September 2023, he received a charge sheet outlining five counts of misconduct related to the tender. Disciplinary hearings began on 19 September 2023, chaired by presiding officer Matome Mashao. The process hit roadblocks early: timelines for document sharing were agreed upon, with hearings scheduled for 1 to 7 November 2023. However, the municipality tried to add six more                                             charges on 1 November, causing delays as Lebusa’s team objected and sought time to review new evidence.                  

                                            Tensions escalated by late November. On 28 November 2023, Lebusa referred the matter to the Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration (CCMA) under Section 188A(11) of the Labour Relations Act, requesting a halt to the proceedings while claiming protection as a potential whistleblower. The municipality countered by seeking to end the hearing, accusing Lebusa of using “dilatory tactics” to stall progress. On 7 December 2023, Mashao ruled in the municipality’s favour, terminating the disciplinary                                             process without resolving the charges.                  

                                            Swift Termination and Legal Challenge                  

                                            Events moved quickly after the ruling. On the same day, the Acting Municipal Manager issued a “Notice of Intention to Terminate,” giving Lebusa only 24 hours to respond and explain why his contract should not be ended immediately. The notice shifted focus from the original misconduct claims to Lebusa’s alleged delays in the hearing. Lebusa submitted his response on 8 December 2023 at 15:22, but the Municipal Council had already convened from 12:00 to 13:57 that day and decided to terminate his employment ri                                            ght away, invoking clauses 17.4 and 17.5 of his contract for breach of conduct and serious misconduct.                  

                                            Lebusa received the formal “Notice of Termination” on 11 December 2023, which confirmed the council’s resolution and declared his position vacant. The municipality soon advertised for a new CFO, signalling a clean break. Feeling wronged, Lebusa launched a High Court application in early 2024 (Case No. 532/2024), split into two parts: Part A for urgent interim relief to stop the termination, and Part B to declare it unlawful.                  

                                            The main hearing for Part B occurred on 10 February 2025, with Lebusa represented by Advocate C.D. Pienaar from Matlho Attorneys, and the respondents—Metsimaholo Local Municipality, its Speaker, Acting Municipal Manager, Municipal Council, and Mashao—by Advocates C. Muza and M. Nduli from Ntleru Inc. Attorneys. Mashao did not actively oppose.                  

                                            Key Findings from the Initial Judgement                  

                                            In the April 29, 2025 ruling, the court dissected the process and found multiple flaws:                  

                                            •  The council’s decision on 8 December 2023 was premature, made before Lebusa’s response deadline, breaching the audi alteram partem rule—ensuring both sides are heard.                  

                                            •  The termination was mischaracterised; the council’s resolution was final, not a mere implementation by the Acting Municipal Manager.                  

                                            •  It violated contract clause 17.7, which requires compliance with statutory laws like the Local Government: Disciplinary Regulations for Senior Managers, 2010, and principles of natural justice.                  

                                            •  Grounds for summary dismissal were unjustified, as no fair hearing occurred, and the shift to “dilatory tactics” lacked evidence.                  

                                            The court ordered:                  

                                            •  The council’s approval of Mashao’s ruling declared unlawful and set aside.                  

                                            •  The 8 December 2023 termination decision invalid.                  

                                            •  The 11 December 2023 termination set aside.                  

                                            •  Restoration of the status quo ante, effectively reinstating Lebusa.                  

                                            •  Costs against the respondents on Scale B, covering counsel fees.                  

                                            This judgement emphasised that unlawful terminations differ from unfair ones under labour law, and the CCMA lacks jurisdiction here, confirming the High Court’s role.                  

                                            Appeal Bid Rejected: No Prospects of Success                  

                                            The municipality sought leave to appeal, arguing errors in fact interpretation, contract provisions, and jurisdiction. They claimed the court overlooked the CCMA referral’s impact and that the ruling’s date was misstated (7 vs. 8 December 2023). However, today’s judgement dismissed these points.                  

                                            The court clarified that the date error was minor and did not affect the ruling’s identification. It found no misdirection, no new facts, and no basis to challenge the unlawfulness. The appeal lacked merit, as the municipality failed to show violations of natural justice were addressed. Clause 19 of the contract, suggesting CCMA jurisdiction, was deemed irrelevant for unlawful terminations.                  

                                            Costs were again awarded against the municipality on Scale B, adding to its financial burden. The judges noted that the core findings on procedural fairness remain unchallenged.                  

                                            Leadership Changes and Ongoing Challenges                  

                                            Amid the dispute, Metsimaholo appointed new leaders on 4 April 2024: Basi Motloung as Municipal Manager (effective 1 April 2024) and Fikile Mareka as CFO. Motloung, with over 20 years in municipal roles, had acted in the position since July 2023. Mareka, boasting 16 years in finance and clean audit achievements, was praised for her expertise. Executive Mayor Cllr Jack Malindi voiced support, hoping for improved governance.                  

                                            Lebusa’s reinstatement now complicates this, potentially displacing Mareka and requiring the municipality to navigate compensation or settlements. The case exposes deeper issues: Metsimaholo has faced repeated audits highlighting financial irregularities, poor service delivery, and corruption probes. The security tender scandal, part of a broader pattern in South Africa’s 257 municipalities, involved claims of manipulated bids and political interference, leading to Zwane’s ousting and calls for stricter ove                                            rsight.
