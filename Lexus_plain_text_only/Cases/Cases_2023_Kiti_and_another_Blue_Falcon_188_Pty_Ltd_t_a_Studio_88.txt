



Kiti and another / Blue Falcon 188 (Pty) Ltd t/a Studio 88 [2023] 10 BALR 1169 (CCMA)
Arbitration Law Reports

Division: Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration
Case No: FSBF2278-23
Date: 16/07/2023
Before: V Basholo, Commissioner
Keywords


Referral in terms of section 191(5)(a)(i) of the LRA
Dismissal — Substantive fairness — Collective misconduct — Entire staff of retail store dismissed for shrinkage without sufficient proof that each was responsible for loss — Dismissal unfair.

Editor’s Summary


The applicants, both shop assistants, were dismissed after the respondent discovered that the store at which they worked had experienced a stock loss of more than R50 000. Both denied that they were in any way responsible for the loss and sought reinstatement.

The Commissioner noted that the respondent had relied on the notion of “derivative misconduct” to justify the dismissal of the applicants and two other employees at the store. There were insufficient grounds for holding that every member of staff was responsible for misconduct. Each employee might have been responsible for checking on stock, but they were not necessarily responsible for the loss. 

The applicants were reinstated with full retrospective effect.

Award

Details of hearing and representation
The arbitration hearing was held under the auspices of the Commission for Conciliation, Mediation & Arbitration (the “Commission”) in terms of section 191 of the Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 (the “LRA”), as amended and the award is issued in terms of section 138(7) of the LRA.
This is an arbitration award between Tshepo Mosesi Simon Kiti and one other, the applicant, and Blue Falcon 188 (Pty) Ltd t/a Studio 88, the respondent. The arbitration hearing was held on 6 July 2023 at the CCMA House in Bloemfontein. 
The applicants represented themselves, whilst the respondent was represented by Mr LS Hardy, the GEO Official.
The hearing was conducted in English and digitally recorded and written hand notes were taken. The interpreter was Mr Rodney Boikanya. 
[2023] 10 BALR 1169 (CCMA) at 1170

Issue to be decided
The matter before me was to determine whether the dismissal of the applicants was both substantively and procedurally fair or not. If the dismissal was unfair, I have to determine the appropriate remedy.

Background to the issue
Mr Tshepo Mosesi Simon Kiti was employed on 1 March 2023 as a Sales Assistant. He earned a salary of R5 888,55 per month. Ms Mammatli Makoae was employed on 1 April 2022 as a Sales Assistant. She earned a salary of R11 900 per month. The applicants were working six days per week. The respondent is in retail and selling clothing at Mimosa Mall store in Bloemfontein.
There were four employees at the store including the applicants. After the stock counting for the period of 1 February 2023 to 5 April 2023 it was discovered that there was stock loss in the amount of R46 875,39 at cost and R93 750,78 at retail. 
The applicants were charged for Work Output in that they were directly responsible for substantial stock loss of R46 975,39 at cost and R93 750,78 at retail. The second charge was for Gross Negligence in that they failed to look after the stock in the shop where they were stationed. They were suspended on 5 April 2023 and the disciplinary hearing was held on 17 April 2023. The parties could not specify the date of the dismissal and according to the document bundle “R” page 19 the chairperson finalised the disciplinary hearing on 20 April 2023 (it appeared they were dismissed on 20 April 2023).
All employees were dismissed on the basis of derivative and collective misconduct in that they were all responsible for the stock loss. Mr Kiti argued that he was only at the respondent for one month when there was stock loss and he was not responsible for the stock loss. Ms Makoae argued that she was constantly doing spot checks and she was not responsible for the stock loss. She had reported to management that an employee, Siphiwe was taking stock. 
Procedural fairness was not in dispute. The applicants sought reinstatement.

Survey of evidence and argument
The respondent submitted a bundle of documents “R” (pages 1–40) and the applicant (Ms Makoae) submitted bundle “A” seven pages.

Respondent’s case
Mr Reginald Rathifili, the Regional Manager, testified in short amongst others as follows:
The applicants did not challenge that there was stock loss of R46 975,39 at cost and R93 750,78 at retail. The policy and procedure state that the manager must do 20 spot checks per day from 

Monday to Friday and this was not done. He never received any reports of missing stock.

Staff members were not allowed to take stock out of the shop and they took caps and went to a party. Employees were aware of the policies and that they were directly responsible for the stock loss. The applicants did not follow the policies and procedures and this resulted in the stock loss.
The applicants were grossly negligent and they could not be trusted and the sanction is dismissal and there was no alternative sanction. The company accepts stock loss of 0.05% and anything more is substantial and leads to a disciplinary hearing.
Under cross-examination it was put to the witness that she (Makoae) 3 February 2023 she reported to him about the cap she saw on somebody [sic]. She also told him that Siphiwe was taking stock from the shop. It was further put to him that the respondent did nothing when she reported the stock that was taken out of the store.
The witness responded that Ms Makoae reported that stock was taken out of the store. He went to the store and counted stock and all employees were issued with final written warnings but it was before 1 February 2023.

Applicant’s case
Mr Tshepo Mosesi Simon Kiti, the first applicant, testified amongst others that he arrived at the store on 1 March 2023 and he was not responsible for the stock loss from 1 February 2023 to 4 April 2023. The General Manager arrived and did spot checks and there was already missing stock when he (applicant) arrived. The Regional Manager told him not to worry and that he was not part of the missing stock and he called other staff members. 
Under cross-examination it was put to him that he was responsible for the stock loss and that when he did the spot checks, he did not inform the Regional Manager about the missing stock, he was negligent.
Ms Mammatli Makoae, the second applicant, testified amongst others as follows:
Had the respondent acted on time there would not have been such a loss. They were doing spot checks three times a week. On 3 February 2023 she saw the beige cap missing and she reported it to the Regional Manager. She reported to the Regional Manager that Siphiwe was taking stock out of the shop. The Regional Manager came several times at the store in February 2023 and March 2023 to do spot checks and take stock count. 
The stock which went missing amounted to R1 200 and on 27 March 2023 the manager, Jojina, told them to pay for the missing stock and she refused.
She presented a WhatsApp message sent to her by the Regional Manager (Reggie) where he stated that “Just keep your head upkeep pushing n making sure the shop sells, Siphiwe was selfish he 

almost got u all fired.” Another message from Reggie dated 31 March 2023 stated, “Don’t stress I know you warned us about all this but will make sure ur job is safe.”

She did her part and reported that stock was taken out of the store but the respondent did not investigate and did nothing. She has been in retail from 2008 and has never been charged for stock loss. The dismissal was unfair and she has a daughter to support, she takes care of her family and she pays rent.
Under cross-examination it was put to her that she was supposed to do spot checks every day. They were given a chance to look at the variance and they signed it. It was further put to her that they were on a final written warning and an ultimatum.
In response the applicant stated that they would be told by the manager when to do spot checks. They were told to sign the variance and they were not given a chance to look at what was the reason for the variance.
In closing the respondent submitted that it was team misconduct and that four employees were charged and dismissed. The respondent could not point to an employee and the employees could not point to who was responsible. Collectively they did not look after the stock and they did not follow policies and procedures. They did not act in the best interest of the company and the loss was in excess of 9% and they were on a final written warning and ultimatum. The respondent referred dismissal as per derivative and collective misconduct cited the case of Chauke and others v Lee Service Centre CC t/a Leeson Motors (1998) 19 ILJ 1441 (LAC) [also reported at [1998] JOL 3624 (LAC) – Ed].

Analysis of evidence and argument
In terms of item 2 of the Code of Good Practice on Dismissal “a dismissal is unfair if it is not effected for a fair reason and in accordance with a fair procedure, even if it complies with any notice period in a contract of employment or in legislation governing employment”. The absence of a valid reason for the dismissal is obviously unfair. It was alleged that there was a charge of misconduct levelled against the applicant. According to item 7 of the Code of Good Practice on Dismissal, any person who is determining whether a dismissal for misconduct is unfair should consider –
whether or not the employee contravened a rule or standard regulating conduct in, or of relevance to, the workplace; and
if a rule or standard was contravened, whether or not –
the rule was a valid or reasonable rule or standard;
the employee was aware, or could reasonably be expected to have been aware, of the rule or standard;
the rule or standard has been consistently applied by the employer; and
dismissal was an appropriate sanction for the contravention of the rule or standard.  
[2023] 10 BALR 1169 (CCMA) at 1173
Section 188 of the Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 determines that a dismissal will be fair if the reason for the dismissal is fair and a fair procedure was followed, furthermore if it relates to conduct, capacity or operational requirements. In terms of section 138(6) Schedule 8 of the Labour Relations Act, 1995.
It was common cause that four employees responsible for the stock were dismissed. I find it important to separate the applicant’s findings as follows:
Mr Kiti was employed at the store on 1 March 2023 and the missing stock was from 1 February 2023 to 5 April 2023. He was in the store for a month and about four days when there was stock loss. He stated that when he arrived there was already stock loss, he was told that he need not worry and that he was not involved in the missing stock. I find it probable that when he arrived, there was already stock loss and he probably was not responsible for the stock loss. The respondent on these bases dismissed all employees on the basis of group misconduct as cited in the above judgment of Chauke and others v Lee Service Centre t/a Leeson Motors (1998) 19 ILJ 1441 (LAC) [also reported at [1998] JOL 3624 (LAC) – Ed].
Ms Makoae stated that she had reported to the Regional Manager (Reggie) that stock was taken out of the store by Siphiwe and even Reggie sent her WhatsApp messages that “Just keep your head upkeep pushing n making sure the shop sells, Siphiwe was selfish he almost got u all fired.” And another message saying, “Don’t stress I know you warned us about all this but will make sure ur job is safe.” Reggie (Mr Rathifithi – the witness) did not dispute these messages. It was evident that stock was taken out of the store resulting in stock loss and management was informed and apparently management did not investigate Siphiwe to verify the allegations of Ms Makoae (“whistleblower”) but instead the respondent stated that all employees were given final written warnings on 1 February 2023 and the stock loss they were dismissed for was from 1 February 2023 to 5 April 2023. The respondent on these bases dismissed all employees on the basis of group misconduct as cited in the above judgment of Chauke and others v Lee Services Centre CC t/a Leeson Motors (1998) 19 ILJ 1441 (LAC) [also reported at [1998] JOL 3624 (LAC) – Ed].
Given the evidence presented it was evident that the applicant informed the respondent as it is required and confirmed by the very same judgment of Chauke and others v Lee Services Centre CC t/a Leeson Motors (1998) 19 ILJ 1441 (LAC) [also reported at [1998] JOL 3624 (LAC) – Ed] which the respondent relied on, that an employee has a duty to assist the employer in bringing the perpetrator to book if an employee has information concerning those involved in the misconduct. 
I find that the respondent failed to present sufficient grounds for inferring that the entire group is either responsible or involved in the misconduct in  
[2023] 10 BALR 1169 (CCMA) at 1174
  some way or the other. I find that the matter of Chauke and others v Lee Services Centre CC t/a Leeson Motors (supra) was not meant to “paint everyone with the same brush” without investigating where there are a number of employees, but it is the responsibility of the respondent to investigate and find the perpetrator. 
I find that the applicants were responsible for the stock, but they were not responsible for the loss of stock. There was stock loss and the respondent had information to verify and therefore the matter of Chauke and others v Lee Services Centre CC t/a Leeson Motors (supra) is not applicable in this case. The dismissal of the applicants was substantively unfair. The applicants sought reinstatement and I have no reasons to deviate from their request.
The applicants were dismissed on 20 April 2023 and they must report for duty on 1 August 2023, therefore their accrued income/back-pay is calculated as follows:
Tshepo Mosesi Simon Kiti
Salary was R5 888,55 per month and working six days per week
R5 888,55/4.33 = R1 359.94 per week
R1 359,94/6 = R226,66 per day
20 April 2023 to 30 April 2023 = 9 days x R226,66 = R2 039,94
1 May 2023 to 31 July 2023 = 3 months x R5 888,55 = R17 665,65
Total accrued income is R19 705.59 (R2 039,94 + R17 665,65)
Mammatli Makoae 
Salary was R11 900 per month and working six days per week
R11 900/4.33 = R2 748,27 per week
R2 748,27/6 = R458,04 per day
20 April 2023 to 30 April 2023 = 9 days x R458,04 = R4 122,36
1 May 2023 to 31 July 2023 = 3 months x R11 900 = R35 700
Total accrued income is R39 822,36 (R4 122,36 + R35 700)

Award
The dismissal of the applicant, Tshepo Mosesi Simon Kiti and one other, by the respondent, Blue Falcon 188 (Pty) Ltd t/a Studio 88, was procedurally fair but substantively unfair. 
The respondent, Blue Falcon 188 (Pty) Ltd t/a Studio 88, is ordered to reinstate the applicants, Tshepo Mosesi Simon Kiti and Mammatli Makoae, on 1 August 2023 on the same terms and conditions of employment as they were before their dismissal and without any loss of income and benefits.
The respondent is further ordered to pay the applicant, Tshepo Mosesi Simon Kiti, accrued income/back-pay in the amount of R19 705,59 on or before 31 July 2023 into his bank account known to the respondent. 
[2023] 10 BALR 1169 (CCMA) at 1175
The respondent is further ordered to pay the applicant, Mammatli Makoae, accrued income/back-pay the amount of R39 822,36 on or before 31 July 2023 into her bank account known to the respondent.
The applicants, Tshepo Mosesi Simon Kiti and Mammatli Makoae, must report for duty on 1 August 2023.
The above amounts in paragraphs 26 and 27 will accrue interest in terms of section 143(2) of the LRA should the respondent not comply with the award on the stated date.
