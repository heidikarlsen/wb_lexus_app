



Rahaman / WDS Global [2004] 12 BALR 1542 (CCMA)
Arbitration Law Reports

Division: CCMA, Johannesburg
Case No: GA24145-04
Date: 12/08/2004
Before: N Maseko, Commissioner (CCMA)
Keywords


Application in terms of section 191(5)(a)(iv) of the LRA
Unfair labour practice — Demotion — Employee failing to prove that he was ever promoted to post from which he claimed to have been demoted — Unfair labour practice not proved.
Unfair labour practice — Final warning — Employer justifiably issuing final warning to employee who traded on his own account.

Editor’s Summary


The applicant was demoted and given a final warning after the respondent accused him of running a business for his own account while in its employment. The applicant denied that he was trading in his name and for profit. The respondent claimed that the applicant had placed himself in a position where his own interests conflicted with that of the company, and that he had been given a final warning for this. However, the respondent denied that the applicant had been demoted.

The commissioner found that the applicant had run a personal account for purposes of purchasing and selling computer equipment. Moreover, he had failed to prove that he was ever promoted to the post from which he claimed to have been demoted. While the respondent should not have issued the final warning without first giving the applicant a hearing, this omission was not so serious as to render its conduct an unfair labour practice.

The application was dismissed.

Award

Details of the case

The arbitration hearing in respect of this matter took place at the CCMA on 11 August 2004. The applicant was in attendance and represented by his attorney, this being Mrs Susan Tricker. Its Manager, Mr David Horwitz, on the other hand represented the respondent. 

Nature of the dispute

The applicant alleges that he has been unfairly treated in the sense that he had been wrongly accused of running a private business. As a consequence thereof, so the applicant alleges, he had been demoted and issued with a final written  
[2004] 12 BALR 1542 (CCMA) at 1543
warning. It is on the basis of these events that he lodged a case of an Unfair Labour Practice. 

Duty to begin

The applicant has the duty to begin in this regard. He thus began with his case. 

The applicant’s evidence

Mr Rahaman testified that he is a call centre agent, and that his previous position was that of MIS. In relation to his job function, he testified that he is responsible for setting up software, solving problems, maintaining the server and supporting the company server. He testified further that he has an account with a private company known as ADX, which sells computers. This, he testified, was an account he used to purchase articles for himself and his close friends. 

He states that he is not trading on the said account even though it is on a business name. Rahaman testified that he had advised the respondent about the existence of his account. He purchased an article from ADX in small quantity and not in bulks.

As to why he had this account, he testified that he could buy articles much cheaper as compared to other stores. He therefore concluded that he did not make profit out of this account. He testified that it does not therefore comprehend why would the respondent allege that he was trading on the ADX account. He testified that he had also purchased articles for the respondent on the said account. 

Rahaman testified that he had been issued with a warning which was not preceded by any process, and that he was thereafter relieved from some of his duties. This action, according to him, was said to have been occasioned by his alleged conflict of interest. On whether he had indeed been appointed to an MIS position, he testified that he had attended an interview and that led to him being promoted. During cross-examination Mr Rahaman contended that being an Administrator and Manager meant the same thing. 

When asked to produce proof that he had been promoted to an MIS position, he testified that there was no such contract. In relation to whether he had received remuneration in respect of his promotion, he testified that he did not receive such remuneration, but he had challenged the respondent on this. Insofar as the MIS position which were advertised are concerned, Mr Rahaman testified that he had applied for such a position, he later contended that he did not apply for such a position because he believed that he had already occupied the said position. 

Instead, he testified, he had been issued with a warning and the position was later advertised. When asked as to whether he was an MIS administrator or Manager he insisted that this meant the same thing. 

The respondent’s evidence

Mr Horwitz testified that what led to a dispute was that the applicant was not comfortable with a written warning. He insisted that the applicant was never  
[2004] 12 BALR 1542 (CCMA) at 1544
appointed to the MIS position, and in fact he had performed some of the functions of an MIS position, but was never formally appointed to the said position. 

He testified that if there had been changes in his job function, that he could have been advised accordingly. In relation to his business activities, Horwitz testified that the company was not concerned about whether Rahaman was making profit from his ADX account. The company was only concerned about the conflict of interest. He testified for instance that there was a call made from the call centre by Mr Rahaman where he procured or attempted to procure articles. 

He further made arrangements to collect those articles during working hours. He testified that the company had been very lenient in not dismissing Rahaman. Horwitz testified further that the applicant had in fact sold computer equipment to his colleague and there is proof to that effect. He insisted that in relation to the claim that he had been demoted that no such demotion had been effected. 

Analysis of evidence

Section 186(2) of the Labour Relations Act defines Unfair Labour Practice as follows, 

“Any unfair act or omission that arises between an employer and an employee involving – 

 (a) Unfair conduct by the employer relating to the promotion, demotion, probation (excluding disputes about dismissals for a reason relating to probation) or training of an employee or relating to the provision of benefits to an employee;

 (b) The unfair suspension of an employee or any other unfair disciplinary action short of dismissal in respect of an employee;

 (c) A failure or refusal by an employer to reinstate or re-employ a former employee in terms of any agreement; and

 (d) An occupation detriment, other than dismissal, in contravention of the Protected Disclosures Act 26 of 2000, on account of the employee having made a protected disclosure defined in that Act.” 


In view of the above definition I must determine whether the respondent has indeed committed an Unfair Labour Practice. Evidence before me suggests that Mr Rahaman had a business account, which was active. 

Although he claimed that the account was not used for business purposes, it is apparent from evidence presented before me that the account was for business purpose. Rahaman did not challenge evidence by the respondent that he for instance sold computer hardware to a colleague. In relation to his claim that he had been promoted, I find his evidence to be very contradictory. 

At one stage he claimed to have applied for a position and that he went for an interview and later appointed. He further testified that he had performed functions of an MIS manager or administrator. He however failed to demonstrate that after he had been promoted he received remuneration in line with his position.

Rahaman also failed to prove that he had indeed challenged the respondent’s failure to effect remuneration that is in line with his promotion. On the basis of this, I am not satisfied with Mr Rahaman’s evidence. It is therefore my view that no Unfair Labour Practice had been committed by the respondent. In fact I am  
[2004] 12 BALR 1542 (CCMA) at 1545
inclined to agree with Mr Horwitz that the respondent has been lenient towards Mr Rahaman. 

Award

On the basis of what I have said above, the applicant has failed to prove that the respondent had indeed committed an Unfair Labour Practice. The applicant’s case must therefore be dismissed. Although it was wrong for Horwitz to issue him with a warning before formally charging him, I do not see this omission to have been material under the circumstances. 

No cases were referred to in the above award.
