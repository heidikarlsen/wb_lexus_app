Whistleblower at CCMA gets fired

Reported on: 28 November 2000

An internal auditor at the Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration, who raised the alarm about public funds that have allegedly gone missing, was fired on Friday. 

The allegations by Ms Jabu Mogadime are currently being investigated by the police commercial crime unit in Johannesburg. 

Ms Mogadime alleges that CCMA director Ms Thandi Orleyn used R150 000 in public funds for her own private affairs and that certain other officials at the CCMA had tried to suppress her investigations into the alleged corruption. 

Mogadime has produced a dossier in which she claims that Orleyn used public funds to travel overseas for the Zenex Foundation, of which she is a board member. 

"She travelled on CCMA expenses and on her return promised to pay back the amount which she used but failed to do," Mogadime said. 

Police have also been given an earlier report by Mogadime in which she alleges that Orleyn manipulated the CCMA's financial regulations to hire Advocate Norman Arendse to do legal work for the commission while he was still working as a senior commissioner for the CCMA. 

Orleyn allegedly paid Arendse R135 774 for 20 days work, apart from the salary he earned from his position as commissioner. 

"For the first 10 days the director (Orleyn) paid Arendse R5 000 a day, plus a daily out­of­town allowance of R300. 

"For the remaining 10 days the director increased Arendse's fee from R5 000 to R8 000 a day. 

"Effectively Arendse earned R135 774 in 20 days," Mogadime said in her report. 

CCMA spokesman Mr S'Thembele Tshwete said the commission was concerned at Mogadime's conduct, which appeared to be aimed at bringing the institution into disrepute. 

He said there were a few individuals in the CCMA who had chosen to go public in an attempt to damage the commission's name.
